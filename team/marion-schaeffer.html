Biography & Research Focus

Structuring knowledge so meaning
becomes computable and auditable.

Marion works at the intersection of applied mathematics, artificial intelligence, and cognitive science. She holds an engineering degree in Applied Mathematics and a PhD in Artificial Intelligence, complemented by academic training in cognitive sciences.

Her work addresses a question at the core of Merlin Intelligence: how does knowledge acquire formal structure? This leads her to knowledge representation — graphs, ontologies, and the formal `OWL/RDFS/SKOS` architectures through which domain causality becomes computable — and to the modern systems operating within those architectures: large language models, retrieval-augmented generation, and governed agentic pipelines.

A central focus of her research is Ontology Learning — the methods by which conceptual hierarchies and typed causal relations can be extracted, induced, and refined iteratively from domain corpora rather than authored solely by hand. Within Merlin Intelligence, this research powers olaf (Ontology Learning Agentic Framework), our open-source MCP server coupling Oxigraph triplestore reasoning with Qdrant semantic indexing, as well as the formal macroeconomic ontology grounding our Equilibrium Portfolio Series.

She pays particular attention to explainability — to the mathematical and architectural conditions under which an AI system can be inspected, audited, and held to account. Across academic collaborations and applied industrial deployments, she brings both the formal rigour of the researcher and the operational judgment of the practitioner.

The question is not whether a system produces the right answer. The question is whether we can understand why — and correct it when we cannot.
